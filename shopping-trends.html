01. Project Overview

This project analyses 3,900 customer records to understand shopping patterns and purchasing behaviour. The analysis explores product categories, seasons, customer demographics and purchase preferences to identify useful business insights.

02. Project Objectives

  • Understand sales performance across product categories.
  • Compare sales across different seasons.
  • Explore customer demographics and product preferences.
  • Examine discounts, subscriptions and purchase behaviour.
  • Present findings to support business decisions.

05. Key Findings

  • Clothing is the top-performing category: Clothing recorded the highest category sales at $104,264, while Outerwear recorded the lowest at $18,524.
  • Fall is the strongest season: Fall recorded the highest seasonal sales at $60,018, while Summer recorded the lowest at $55,777.
  • Male customers contributed more total sales: Male records accounted for $157,890, compared with $75,191 for Female records.
  • Medium (M) is the most popular size: Size M appeared in 1,755 records, whereas XL appeared in only 429 records.
  • Non-subscribed customers contributed more sales: Non-subscribed records accounted for $170,436, compared with $62,645 for subscribed records.
  • Discounts were associated with lower average purchase amounts: Records with no discounts averaged $60.13, compared with $59.28 for discounted records. This does not prove that discounts caused lower spending.
  • Overall customer spending and satisfaction: The dataset recorded $233,081 in total purchase value, an average purchase amount of $59.76, and an average review rating of 3.75/5.

06. Business Recommendations

  • Strengthen the leading category: Keep Clothing product availability healthy and explore cross-selling with Accessories, the second-largest category by total purchase amount.
  • Investigate Outerwear: Review product range, seasonal fit, stock availability, and customer feedback before deciding on promotions.
  • Use seasonal planning: Fall has the highest purchase value in the dataset; evaluate campaign timing and compare results with Summer.
  • Optimize size-level inventory: M has the largest record count and XL the smallest. Validate demand by category and item before making inventory decisions.
  • Improve subscription conversion: Non-subscribed records contribute more total purchase value. Test member benefits and track conversion, repeat purchase rate, and incremental revenue.
  • Measure promotions properly: Discount and promo-code records have lower average purchase amounts in this dataset; this does not prove the promotions caused lower spending. Compare margins and a suitable control group before changing strategy.
  • Monitor customer feedback: Use the 3.75/5 average rating as a baseline and examine lower-rated records by category and shipping type.
  • Build a repeatable reporting process: Refresh the source data and recalculate KPIs consistently across Excel, SQL, Python, Power BI, and Tableau.

07. Business Impact

Seasonal Strategy
